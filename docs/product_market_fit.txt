Product-Market Fit: What It Is and How to Find It

Product-market fit (PMF) means being in a good market with a product that can satisfy that market. Marc Andreessen coined the term, and it remains the single most important milestone for an early-stage startup.

The Sean Ellis Test is the most common way to measure PMF: survey your users and ask "How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?" If more than 40% say "very disappointed," you likely have PMF. Below 40% means you need to keep iterating.

Signs you have PMF:
- Users come back without being reminded
- Word-of-mouth is your biggest acquisition channel
- You struggle to keep up with demand
- Churn is low and users complain loudly when features are removed
- People tell friends about your product unprompted

How to find PMF:
1. Start with a narrow, specific customer segment. Don't try to serve everyone.
2. Talk to 50+ potential customers before building anything.
3. Find the problem that makes people say "yes, this is a huge pain."
4. Build the smallest thing that solves that pain.
5. Get 10 users who love it before acquiring 100 who are lukewarm.

Common mistakes:
- Confusing activity (signups, page views) with retention and love
- Expanding to new segments before nailing the first one
- Mistaking polite feedback for genuine enthusiasm
- Optimizing for growth before confirming retention

YC partners often say: if you have to ask whether you have PMF, you probably don't. When you have it, it feels obvious — customers pull the product out of you rather than you pushing it at them.

The pivot decision: if after 6-12 months of focused effort you cannot get past 20-25% on the Ellis test with any customer segment, consider a pivot. Change either the customer, the problem, or the solution — not all three at once.
