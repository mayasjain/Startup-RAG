How to Raise a Seed Round

Fundraising is a necessary evil for most startups. The goal is to raise enough money to reach your next meaningful milestone, not to maximize the amount raised.

When to raise:
- You have a clear hypothesis you want to test
- You have some early signal (users, revenue, or a compelling insight)
- You can articulate what you'll do with the money and what it buys you
- Raising before any of these is extremely hard and dilutes you heavily

How much to raise:
- Target 18-24 months of runway
- Be specific: list your hires and costs, don't just guess
- Raise 20% more than you think you need
- Typical pre-seed: $500K–$1.5M. Seed: $1.5M–$4M.

The process:
1. Build a list of 50–100 investors (angels, pre-seed funds, seed funds)
2. Get warm introductions wherever possible — cold outreach has a very low hit rate
3. Run a tight process: compress all meetings into 2-3 weeks to create momentum
4. Get a lead investor first — others follow
5. Once you have a term sheet, close quickly

What investors look for at seed:
- Team: domain expertise, execution speed, cofounder dynamics
- Market: large enough to build a big company
- Insight: do you know something others don't?
- Traction: any evidence customers want this

The pitch narrative:
- Start with the problem (make them feel the pain)
- Explain your solution and why it works
- Show traction or validation
- Describe the market size
- Explain why you and your team are uniquely suited to win

Common mistakes:
- Pitching too early without any validation
- Asking for NDAs (investors won't sign them)
- Sending a deck cold without context
- Optimizing for valuation over finding the right partner
- Raising too little and running out of runway before hitting milestones

SAFE notes vs priced rounds: most seed rounds now use SAFEs (Simple Agreement for Future Equity). They're faster and cheaper to close than priced rounds. Use a post-money SAFE with a valuation cap. Standard caps are $8M–$15M for pre-seed, $15M–$25M for seed depending on traction.
